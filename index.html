Do I need a GS1 number to print a UPC label?

The barcode is free; the number may not be. A UPC-A barcode is just a printed encoding of a 12-digit number. This tool makes that barcode for you at no cost. But if you want your product to scan in a retail store or on Amazon, the number inside the barcode has to be one you are authorised to use — that means a GTIN / company prefix issued by GS1, purchased through GS1 US in the United States.

What does a GS1 US UPC number cost?

Generating a barcode image here is free. Licensing a product number from GS1 US is a separate cost. A single GS1 US GTIN costs $30 with no annual renewal fee. Company prefix options provide capacity for multiple product numbers:

Numbers you invent yourself are for internal use only — inventory, warehouse, in-house tracking. They must not go on a product you sell to the public: an invented number can collide with a number another brand already owns, and the retailer will reject the listing or charge the item to the wrong company.

How to read the 12 digits: the first 1–11 digits are the company prefix plus the item reference, and the 12th digit is a check digit the scanner verifies. Enter 11 digits here and the 12th is computed for you; if you paste all 12, we validate the check digit before drawing.

How to generate a UPC label

For a quick example, enter 03600029145 in the generator and select Generate barcode. The result is 036000291452: the final digit is calculated automatically. This example demonstrates the format; it does not assign the number to your product.

  1. Enter 11 digits. We compute the UPC-A check digit and draw the barcode — PNG, SVG, or straight onto a label sheet.
  2. Pick your label sheet. The PDF is laid out to Avery 5160 / 5163 / 5164 geometry, so what you see is where the labels land when you print at 100% scale.
  3. Print and scan. Print at 100% (no "fit to page"), on matte label stock. Test one sheet with a real scanner before running a full batch.

UPC label questions

Can I enter an existing 12-digit UPC?

Yes. Enter either 11 digits to calculate the final check digit, or all 12 digits to validate an existing UPC-A. Keep leading zeros: they are part of the number. Letters, spaces inside a number, and other barcode formats are rejected.

Does a valid check digit mean I own the number?

No. A check digit verifies the arithmetic of a UPC-A number. It does not confirm ownership, product registration, or retailer acceptance. Use numbers assigned to your business and confirm requirements with the retailer before labeling stock.

How do I prepare a CSV batch?

Use a header row named upc, product, qty. Store UPC values as text in your spreadsheet so leading zeros are preserved. Product names and quantities are optional. Each valid row becomes a product; qty controls copies. The preview displays only the first 20 products, while the PDF includes every accepted row, up to 1,500 labels. Files exceeding 500 rows are rejected so no rows are silently lost.

Should I download PNG, SVG, or PDF?

PNG is a high resolution raster image suitable for documents. SVG keeps the bars as vector shapes that scale cleanly. Both download the first product in an imported batch. PDF places all accepted products on US Letter sheets using the selected Avery layout. Avoid stretching a barcode independently in either direction.

How do I print labels at the correct size?

Choose US Letter paper and print at actual size or 100 percent. Disable fit to page and browser scaling. First print on plain paper and compare it against your label stock. Check the first and last rows for alignment before using adhesive sheets. Printer feed and margin settings can affect placement, so test several pages before a production run.

Why might a printed barcode fail to scan?

Blurry edges, low contrast, glossy reflections, clipping, or insufficient blank space beside the bars can prevent scanning. Keep the white margins intact and use dark ink on a plain white background. Test the exported image on screen and then scan a physical print with the scanner used in your workflow. Higher resolution helps preserve edges but cannot guarantee a successful scan.
