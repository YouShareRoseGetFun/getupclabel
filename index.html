Do I need a GS1 number to print a UPC label?

The barcode is free; the number may not be. A UPC-A barcode is just a printed encoding of a 12-digit number. This tool makes that barcode for you at no cost. But if you want your product to scan in a retail store or on Amazon, the number inside the barcode has to be one you are authorised to use — that means a GTIN / company prefix issued by GS1, purchased through GS1 US in the United States.

What GS1 US charges (published on gs1us.org): a single GTIN is $30 with no annual fee; 10 GTINs are $250 plus $50/year; 100 GTINs are $750 plus $150/year; 1,000 GTINs are $2,500 plus $500/year. A company prefix is what lets you assign numbers yourself within your own range.

Numbers you invent yourself are for internal use only — inventory, warehouse, in-house tracking. They must not go on a product you sell to the public: an invented number can collide with a number another brand already owns, and the retailer will reject the listing or charge the item to the wrong company.

How to read the 12 digits: the first 1–11 digits are the company prefix plus the item reference, and the 12th digit is a check digit the scanner verifies. Enter 11 digits here and the 12th is computed for you; if you paste all 12, we validate the check digit before drawing.

How it works

  1. Enter 11 digits. We compute the UPC-A check digit and draw the barcode — PNG, SVG, or straight onto a label sheet.
  2. Pick your label sheet. The PDF is laid out to Avery 5160 / 5163 / 5164 geometry, so what you see is where the labels land when you print at 100% scale.
  3. Print and scan. Print at 100% (no "fit to page"), on matte label stock. Test one sheet with a real scanner before running a full batch.
